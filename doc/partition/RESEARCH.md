# Hardware-aware mapping research and implementation rationale

Prepared 2026-10-13. Scope: synthesized-netlist analysis and assignment files only. No wrapped netlists, logic replication, retiming, or remote pushes. Backend requirement: **Mt-KaHyPar v1.6.2 only**. The recommendations below distinguish source findings from the proposed RapidWright mapping policy; none constitutes a proof of a globally optimal partition or a prediction of achieved hardware clock frequency.

This note preserves dated research and prototype history. Historical defaults,
test counts, `NOT_REQUESTED` capacity statuses and individual replay metrics
describe those revisions, not the final released results. Local prototype
artifacts are optional provenance and are not shipped or needed to build/run
the standalone tool. Use the [current manual](README.md) for executable options
and the [validation report](VALIDATION_REPORT.tex) with its portable evidence
snapshots for the final J4 matrix and current release checks. Primary academic
and vendor citations below remain the sources for the research claims.

## What the primary evidence establishes

1. **Keep Mt-KaHyPar, but give it the right problem.** Its multilevel framework combines coarsening, FM refinement, and flow refinement; these are strong general-purpose tools, not a hardware timing model. The published evaluation covers over 800 graph/hypergraph instances. Hardware-specific behavior therefore belongs in the graph construction, constraints, directed analysis, and candidate selection around the backend. [Gottesbüren et al., Scalable High-Quality Hypergraph Partitioning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17679).

2. **Topology should participate during optimization.** Heuer's direct mapping method optimizes the sum of net-induced Steiner-tree costs on a weighted target graph, rather than merely permuting already finished partitions. The paper supplies mapping-aware local and flow refinements, and reports a median 7% Steiner-objective improvement over partition-then-map on its VLSI instances. This is evidence for topology-aware optimization; it is not a 7% FPGA frequency claim. [Heuer, ALENEX 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16694).

3. **v1.6.2 has the needed scalar/topology features.** The pinned README documents `-g target.graph -o steiner_tree`, fixed vertices, individual scalar block weights, and quality/deterministic-quality presets. Target graphs are limited to 64 nodes, accommodating 4, 16, 24, and 28 FPGA experiments. Its Windows instructions use MSYS2/MinGW; its pip installation section covers Linux and macOS. Do not substitute v1.7 or assume a native Windows pip wheel. [Pinned v1.6.2 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kahypar/mt-kahypar/v1.6.2/README.md).

4. **Native nonempty blocks do not prove nonempty useful logic.** The v1.6.1 release restored nonempty-block guarantees, inherited by v1.6.2. A block containing only virtual terminals, constants, or zero-resource artifacts can still violate the user's intended occupancy. Check actual mapped logic independently. [v1.6.1 release](https://github.com/kahypar/mt-kahypar/releases/tag/v1.6.1). Steiner compatibility with fixed vertices and individual scalar capacities was corrected in v1.6. [v1.6 release](https://github.com/kahypar/mt-kahypar/releases/tag/v1.6). v1.6.2 fixes excessive memory use in some large-k cases. [v1.6.2 release](https://github.com/kahypar/mt-kahypar/releases/tag/v1.6.2).

5. **A scalar LUT balance is insufficient.** v1.6.2's file-format documentation explicitly excludes multidimensional constraints. Graph IDs in input files are one-based, while backend output partition IDs are zero-based. A METIS target graph must be symmetric with equal reverse-edge costs. [Pinned file formats](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kahypar/mt-kahypar/v1.6.2/mt-kahypar/io/docs/FileFormats.md). A 2026 research extension to Mt-KaHyPar introduces multidimensional rebalancing in a separate `esa2026` branch, based on v1.5.3, outside the required backend version. Its two-dimensional guarantees require bounded node weights and do not generalize directly to arbitrary FPGA resource vectors. The useful transferable idea is allowing intermediate moves that reduce total positive resource overflow, then independently validating final feasibility. [Maas, High-Quality Multi-Constraint Hypergraph Partitioning via Greedy Rebalancing](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28333v1).

6. **Timing requires paths as well as net cuts.** TritonPart combines weighted net cuts, path boundary counts, and path snaking, then propagates the extra cut delay through its timing graph so previously noncritical paths can become important. Its documented snaking factor measures block reentries. Consequently A→B→C has zero reentries even though B is exactly the register-free transit the user dislikes. Both transit and repeated-boundary metrics are required. [TritonPart paper, Sections II and IV](https://vlsicad.ucsd.edu/Publications/Conferences/401/c401.pdf), [OpenROAD partition documentation](https://openroad.readthedocs.io/en/latest/main/src/par/README.html).

7. **Minimize scarce-link pressure, not just total cuts.** The ASP-DAC 2023 partition/TDM framework first reduces the maximum FPGA-pair cut burden and then assigns TDM ratios. Its abstract also notes clock compatibility constraints on wire sharing. This supports reporting bottlenecks per link or pair in addition to global connectivity. [Zheng and Young, ASP-DAC 2023](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10044749). A related routing/TDM paper models source-to-terminal trees and optimizes the maximum group TDM burden, illustrating why a route's total length and its worst critical path are different quantities. [Routing Topology and Time-Division Multiplexing Co-Optimization, 2020](https://waynelin567.github.io/files/FPGA_TDM.pdf).

8. **Strict one-hop constraints can be infeasible.** MFSPart's 2026 formulation combines capacity constraints, configurable maximum hops, driver–sink cut size, net connectivity, and mean topology distance. Connectivity accounts for sharing one transmitted net among multiple sinks on the same FPGA; counting every cell sink as a separate physical transfer exaggerates demand. Its ensemble extension recombines solution structure instead of trusting one seed. [Zhu et al., MFSPart, accepted TCAD author version](https://zhiyaoxie.com/files/TCAD26_MFSPart.pdf).

9. **Vendor public claims support the goals, not a disclosed secret algorithm.** Synopsys publicly describes constraint-driven partitioning, hardware interconnect awareness, timing-driven synthesis, and high-speed multiplexing. A separate public announcement describes using detailed trace delays and selecting multiplexing schemes. Neither exposes enough implementation detail to reproduce ProtoCompiler or prove its partition optimum. Our inference is to model the same relevant information explicitly, not claim algorithmic equivalence. [Synopsys prototyping](https://www.synopsys.com/verification/emulation-prototyping/prototyping.html), [ProtoCompiler announcement](https://news.synopsys.com/2014-04-23-Synopsys-New-ProtoCompiler-Software-Speeds-Time-to-First-Prototype-by-Up-to-3X).

10. **Vivado can provide real timing identity when available.** Its timing-path objects expose launch/capture pins, clock domains, slack, requirement, and datapath delay, supporting richer inputs than EDIF connectivity alone. The implemented sampled-path export uses these source constraints unchanged; post-synthesis timing is still not post-partition routed timing. [AMD Vivado 2025.1 TIMING_PATH reference](https://docs.amd.com/r/2025.1-English/ug912-vivado-properties/TIMING_PATH).

## Proposed mapping policy

This section is an engineering proposal, not a claimed implementation of a cited vendor tool.

Let π(v) be a frontier vertex's FPGA. Preserve a separate directed primitive dependency graph. Let resource load L(i,r) be the sum of resource r on FPGA i, with user capacity C(i,r). Hard requirements are:

- Every mapped vertex has exactly one valid FPGA assignment, all fixed assignments hold, and every requested FPGA contains positive real design logic.
- Every resource has L(i,r) ≤ C(i,r). At minimum count LUTs, FF/latches, BRAM18 equivalents, DSPs, URAM, and LUTRAM/SRL usage separately. A logic-LUT count omits LUTRAM/SRL slice consumption; report resource estimates honestly rather than promising placement feasibility.
- A topology must contain exactly the requested FPGAs and have connected communication paths. If the user supplies a maximum-hop constraint, enforce it explicitly or reject the mapping; a finite distance penalty alone is not enforcement.
- Any unsupported primitive, ambiguous electrical direction, or combinational cycle is reported. Zero detected snakes is only meaningful under the stated analysis coverage.

Choose among feasible candidates using multiple independent measures:

| Measure | Definition / interpretation |
|---|---|
| Cut nets | Number of nets spanning more than one FPGA. |
| Connectivity / transfers | Sum over nets of distinct destination FPGA count, excluding the driver's FPGA. One net feeding 200 sinks on one remote FPGA consumes one logical transfer. |
| Topology tree cost | Sum of weighted net-induced tree costs; strong initial mapping objective. |
| Bottleneck demand | Maximum estimated routed demand / usable physical capacity over links, separately by relevant clock/TDM classes. |
| Maximum register-free crossings | Maximum number of FPGA changes between launch/capture boundaries or declared I/O boundaries. A→B→C contributes two. |
| Maximum minimum-link hops H | Maximum over modeled register-free paths of the sum of shortest physical-link distances between consecutive FPGA owners in the declared selected graph. |
| Maximum minimum relay traversals R | Independent maximum of sum max(0,h−1) along each modeled path; report-only lower bound, distinct from combinational transit primitives. |
| Endpoint excess | For each capture/terminal endpoint, take its maximum crossing count d; sum max(0,d−1). This is not a sum over exponentially many paths. |
| Transit region size | Number of combinational primitives reachable from a foreign-FPGA ingress and able to reach a foreign-FPGA egress without a register while remaining in their FPGA. |
| Reentries | Repeated visits to an FPGA on a register-free witness; catches A→B→A separately. |

A practical default is a constrained tradeoff: preserve resource/fixed/occupancy feasibility; limit transfer growth relative to the reported baseline; improve worst minimum-link hops H, then worst logical FPGA crossings, sampled source timing cost, endpoint excess, and transit size before optimizing soft balance. An explicit transport-delay model instead permits sampled modeled slack and timing cost to lead, followed by H and logical metrics, as detailed below. Keep a Pareto table if candidates disagree. A single weighted sum can exchange one severe timing defect for many small net improvements, so the resulting policy must be explicit and configurable.

Report labels need the same distinction. Java `native_max_crossings`,
`native_transit_primitives` and `timing_cost_native` describe the raw solver
assignment evaluated by this source model. They do not denote Vivado-measured
inter-FPGA timing or a native vendor snake analyzer. Final structural results
come from `after_*`/the selected row; native original-checkpoint utilization
and source STA exports are separate measurements with their own scope.

The default transfer/topology growth allowance is now **10%**, following the user's explicit preference to spend additional cuts when that materially reduces register-free FPGA transit. The earlier 3% threshold was an initial engineering heuristic; the cited research supplies no universal academic justification for either 3% or 10%. These percentages are configurable policy choices, not mathematically optimal constants. Any requested native capacity, logical resource capacities, fixed assignments, and nonempty useful logic remain hard acceptance constraints. Without an explicit transport-delay model, complete audited candidates inside the cut/topology budget are ranked by worst H, worst logical crossings, sampled source timing cost, endpoint excess, then transit primitives, before soft native balance and cut tie breakers.

This policy uses unique driver-to-destination FPGA transfers and weighted shortest-path distance as the two bounded costs. It does not claim that a 10% increase in those counts yields at most 10% extra TDM, compression ratio, or frequency loss: bottleneck links and routed traffic can respond differently. TritonPart motivates evaluating timing paths beyond cut count; MFSPart motivates treating FPGA resource and topology restrictions explicitly. Neither paper establishes a universal conversion from a saved crossing to an acceptable number of extra cuts. The choice should therefore remain visible in the reported budget and candidate tradeoff table. [TritonPart](https://vlsicad.ucsd.edu/Publications/Conferences/401/c401.pdf), [MFSPart](https://zhiyaoxie.com/files/TCAD26_MFSPart.pdf).

The recorded three-FPGA OpenTitan comparison illustrates the tradeoff. Reanalysis of both unchanged assignments with the final memory-pin model gave maximum crossings 7→6, endpoint excess 58,268→46,694, and transit primitives 6,240→4,945. Transfers increased 2,930→3,032 (3.48%), while topology distance decreased 3,638→3,587. Both audits were complete with zero cyclic or blocked nodes. The earlier 3% allowance excluded this candidate; 10% admits a 19.86% endpoint-excess reduction and 20.75% transit reduction under the same hard-cap gate. These are netlist/mapping measurements, not measured hardware clock improvements.
That recorded comparison predates the declared-link path audit and reports
logical crossings; it makes no unmeasured H improvement claim.

### Declared-link path severity

Native Steiner mapping and aggregate weighted driver-to-destination distance
do not bound the longest routed exposure of an individual data path. On a
four-device chain, a single logical 0→3 crossing requires at least three
physical links and two intermediate routing-device traversals. A mapping
can therefore have logical maximum one and still incur relay traffic.
Production now computes BFS minimum-link distances from validated direct
adjacency, separately from weighted routing preferences, and applies the
same sequential-reset DAG program to H and independent R maxima. Positive
symmetric matrices alone are insufficient: supplied hop matrices must
equal BFS distances of their distance-one adjacency. The implicit complete
fallback has no declared physical-hop availability.

H is included in objective ranking, bounded witness severity and the selected
retained-pass quality guard alongside logical and sampled timing measures. R is reported
only and may have a different maximizing path. Neither is the existing
combinational-transit metric. They are lower bounds relative to the selected
declared graph and original modeled launch/capture paths, not actual multicast
routes, congestion, transport-register capture, measured nanoseconds or DUT
frequency. Complete J4 has H=C and R=0, preserving previous ranking and costs.
The exported `worst_combinational_path.tsv` is a C-maximal primitive witness;
it is not guaranteed to attain maximum H on a sparse graph. Full H remains
audited independently, and bounded H-guided search does not change that
exported witness's meaning.
The isolated proof passed 165,888 exhaustive four-node DAG/mapping cases,
1,024 complete-J4 mappings and 256 objective-equivalence assignments; all
98 partition JUnit tests passed against the proposed classes before promotion.

Growth allowances share one reference rather than compounding at each stage. If seed selection spends the allowance relative to its best-cut feasible seed, cleanup retains that reference; outer native-feedback acceptance also uses the first cap-feasible run's reported reference. A test case with a 10-transfer reference and an 11-transfer selected seed demonstrates why this matters: allowing another 10% above 11 would admit a 12-transfer cleanup, exceeding the original bound of 11. Native balance alone also does not trigger early stopping while register-free transit defects remain; the requested iteration budget remains available for path improvements.

### Accepted-quality anchors and explicit quality guards

Quality ranking and hard feasibility serve different purposes. Production defaults to `--quality-guard ranked`: retained greedy/exchange passes and outer feedback acceptance use the declared quality priority, excluding soft native balance and transfer/topology tie breakers. Without an explicit delay model the tuple is (maximum H when available, maximum logical C, sampled timing cost, endpoint excess, transit); with explicit per-logical-crossing delay it is (negative modeled worst sampled slack, sampled timing cost, H, C, endpoint excess, transit). Analysis completeness may not regress. The tuple is compared lexicographically, so a higher-priority improvement can permit a lower-priority regression. Resource/scalar bounds, fixed assignments, useful occupancy, requested native caps and the original global cut/topology budgets remain independent hard checks. `--quality-guard componentwise` preserves the earlier policy, requiring every reference structural/timing measure to remain nonregressing. The Java spelling is `--quality_guard`.

Earlier frozen experiments compared against each iteration's repaired native baseline; changing scalar feedback weights could therefore impose an accidentally stricter secondary bound than the already accepted mapping. Production carries an accepted-quality anchor across feedback iterations instead. This solves a different problem from allowing a declared ranked tradeoff: the anchor chooses the correct published incumbent to compare against; the selected guard states which quality comparison is promised.

The fresh timing-guided OpenTitan k3 trial supplies a concrete example. Its first native-cap-accepted candidate has maximum crossings 5, endpoint excess 77,363, transit 10,096, and sampled timing cost zero. The next feedback iteration starts at 7 / 69,553 / 5,317 / zero and proposes 5 / 57,396 / 5,854 / zero. The proposal improves or preserves every structural and sampled-timing measure of the previously accepted candidate, but seed-relative local rollback rejects it because 5,854 exceeds the new starting seed's transit count 5,317. This is a conservative search limitation, not proof that improving the accepted mapping is infeasible.

The implemented anchor carries the best previously native-cap-accepted mapping through the feedback run. Resource, fixed-assignment, occupancy, native capacity, and shared cut/topology checks remain independent; proposed-pass quality is compared against that accepted anchor instead of promising to dominate every new internal seed. The same-run frontend checks the incumbent mapping, frontier, assignment and native completion hashes before writing source-bound vertex/path/partition rows. It records checkpoint, topology, timing sample, engine and requested capacity identities; the Java parser checks exact frontier coverage and the refiner reevaluates structural/timing metrics under the current model. Newly calibrated scalar limits apply to the proposed result, while the old anchor need not fit those new proxy weights. If no anchor-feasible proposal passes native acceptance, the frontend keeps the previous mapping. The cut/topology reference stays fixed when the quality anchor improves. External or cross-run anchor files require their own trusted native receipt validation; a source-bound TSV alone is not proof of native acceptance.

A recognized solver rejection stating that no proposal satisfies the accepted quality anchor now consumes one attempt rather than ending the requested search budget. Subsequent attempts use new deterministic seed blocks with the last valid calibrated weights and the unchanged incumbent and original growth references. An unsuccessful attempt supplies no new native calibration. Unrecognized tool runtime failures retain the conservative stop-and-preserve behavior. This is bounded search persistence, not a relaxation of acceptance or evidence that a later attempt must improve.

An isolated anchor prototype has now reproduced that actual saved-seed proposal and passed native Vivado acceptance on the unchanged original checkpoint. Its compact mapping has CLB counts 77,980 / 77,985 / 74,825, all below the configured 5,000,000 caps. Native imbalance is 1.3714%, versus 1.0242% for the previous candidate; both satisfy the 3% target. Maximum crossings remain five, endpoint excess falls 25.81%, transit falls 42.02%, transfers fall 3,872→3,617, and topology distance falls 4,745→4,634; sampled timing cost stays zero. Source/checkpoint/inventory hashes remained unchanged, and the generated mapping hash matches the native source-mapping snapshot. This proves a real mapping improvement with verified native area and a small soft-balance tradeoff within target. It does not establish partitioned routed timing. The frozen matrix outputs remain unchanged; the proof was retained in the historical local prototype artifacts (not shipped).

This anchor is an engineering consequence of maintaining a feasible incumbent, not a claimed feature copied from a cited vendor. The example above needs no secondary regression; correcting its comparison anchor already resolves the conflict. Ranked mode separately allows actual secondary regressions when a higher-priority quality measure improves, with the policy and original budgets reported. TritonPart's weighted objectives and MFSPart's multiple resource/topology criteria motivate keeping objective ranking distinct from hard feasibility; neither establishes that componentwise domination of each fresh solver seed is necessary, nor proves this implementation's exact lexicographic policy optimal. [TritonPart](https://vlsicad.ucsd.edu/Publications/Conferences/401/c401.pdf), [MFSPart](https://zhiyaoxie.com/files/TCAD26_MFSPart.pdf).

### Bounded balanced exchanges after greedy refinement

A single-vertex descent can stop at a tight scalar/resource boundary even when exchanging two vertices is feasible. The four-node fixture A→B→A→B has two equal-size full partitions and fixed launch/capture owners: neither individual internal move fits, while a paired exchange gives A→A→B→B and removes two register-free crossings. Production now runs a bounded exchange phase **after** the ordinary greedy phase, initialized from its retained audited best. It does not interleave speculative swaps with the trajectory that established that fallback.

Each sweep considers at most 64 relevant vertices, 16 feasible partners per vertex, 4,096 exact probes and 4 committed exchanges, with 8,000,000 conservative scan units and 65,536 incident indices per neighborhood. Oversized neighborhoods are skipped rather than approximately evaluated. The existing configured pass count bounds each phase separately: up to P greedy sweeps followed by up to P exchange sweeps, with early termination; it is not P combined sweeps. Simultaneous final paired loads must satisfy all six logical resources, configured scalar/per-device capacities, fixed owners and useful occupancy. Objective deltas use the deduplicated union of both vertices' affected nets, structural witnesses and sampled STA paths, with restored trial caches. Declared-topology costs use BFS H, while explicit added nanoseconds remain per logical C.

The full DAG/H and sampled-timing audit remains authoritative after a sweep. A retained exchange must satisfy the selected ranked or componentwise guard against both the original/prior-native quality reference and the retained greedy/best exchange result. It must satisfy the same original global cut/topology references and all final constraints. A rejected trajectory restores the retained best. This matters: an earlier interleaved prototype lost an already good greedy path. A later componentwise k4 prototype rejected endpoint excess 51,575→52,290 despite an improvement elsewhere. That is a recorded result of the conservative policy, not a claim that ranked mode forbids every secondary regression. The separate bounded phase retains a quality-ranked fallback and does not promise to find every beneficial exchange.

## Register-to-register path research and finer boundaries

Rodriguez et al. model registers and I/O separately from combinational dependency DAGs, and add topology-aware worst-path refinement to min-cut partitioning. Their conclusion reports path-cost improvements of 12–40% and cut-size increases ranging from 1.2 to 5 times. These are their benchmark results, not results of this repository, and establish no universal percentage allowance. [A Hypergraph Model and Associated Optimization Strategies for Path Length-Driven Netlist Partitioning, ICCS 2023](https://www.iccs-meeting.org/archive/iccs2023/papers/140750633.pdf), [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-36024-4_50).

A remaining engineering hypothesis here is to expose a bounded number of primitive leaves along the actual worst boundary witnesses when a coarse movable owner blocks a small useful move. Such an experiment must first reproduce the unchanged incumbent on one common finer frontier, preserve scalar totals and original budgets, then pass full global path checks and fresh native validation. The isolated WORK prototypes are not yet a promoted automatic feature. Atomic contraction of entire combinational cones may create oversized clusters; it needs feasibility evidence before adoption. Mt-KaHyPar v1.6.2 remains the sole partitioning backend.

## Exact path audit without path enumeration

On a primitive dependency DAG, give an edge u→v weight 1 if π(u)≠π(v), otherwise 0. Sequential cells have separate launch and capture roles: count the edge entering capture, stop that path, and start an independent outgoing path at crossing count zero. In topological order, the recurrence d(v)=max over predecessors u of d(u)+cross(u,v) gives the maximum crossings to every endpoint. A predecessor pointer supplies a concrete witness. Runtime is O(V+E); it considers all modeled paths, not just a selected timing report.

For transit, propagate an ingress flag forward through same-FPGA combinational arcs and an egress flag backward through those arcs. Their intersection is the set of combinational transit primitives. This detects A→B→C without relying on reentries. A combinational cycle invalidates the DAG certificate: use SCC analysis to identify true cyclic nodes and distinguish downstream nodes blocked by them. Partial acyclic metrics are lower bounds.

The added `CombinationalPathAnalyzer` implements this audit over the supplied graph and exposes bounded endpoint-maximal witnesses for refinement. Its correctness is conditional on the supplied arcs: all-input/all-output cell dependencies overapproximate timing connectivity; module-level connectivity can invent paths and cycles. Exact pin-level timing arcs or input/output reachability summaries are the next accuracy improvement. Registers inside an otherwise combinational module do not make every module input/output pair registered. DSP register configuration, asynchronous RAM paths, latches, clock/reset controls, opaque macros, and top-level I/O homes require explicit modeling.

Two specific primitive hazards deserve regression tests. SRL dynamic address changes select Q after a LUT access delay, so a whole SRL primitive cannot safely be treated as a sequential barrier; its D path and address path have different semantics. [AMD shift-register guidance](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug1387-acap-hardware-ip-platform-dev-methodology/Coding-Shift-Registers-and-Delay-Lines). RAMB cascade multiplexers can select the incoming cascade data instead of local memory data, and UltraRAM cascade registers are optional. A memory's normal synchronous read does not establish that every cascade path is captured. [AMD RAMB cascading](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug573-ultrascale-memory-resources/Cascadable-Block-RAM), [AMD UltraRAM cascading](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/am007-versal-memory/Cascading-UltraRAM-and-Matrix-Configurations).

### Implemented memory and DSP pin policy

`PrimitiveTimingPolicy` separates a trusted memory's original output node from one synthetic sequential `@write_capture` endpoint. Both nodes have the same movable frontier owner; the synthesized netlist, resource census, and hypergraph incidence remain unchanged. SRL D/CE, LUTRAM write data/enables, and write-only address inputs terminate at capture. Asynchronous read addresses continue through the output node. For a multi-port LUTRAM, an address contributes a read dependency only if its corresponding output port is connected: RAM32M ADDRD affects DOD, while RAM32M16/RAM64M8 ADDRH affects DOH. Shared read/write addresses contribute both roles when that read port is used. This avoids falsely connecting a write address to unrelated read ports. Remaining dependencies between used ports are conservative at cell granularity. [AMD RAM32M port reference](https://docs.amd.com/r/2020.2-English/ug1353-versal-architecture-ai-libraries/RAM32M).

Normal BRAM/URAM data, address, and write-enable pins retain synchronous capture even when active cascade data requires a combinational output node. RAMB18E2/RAMB36E2 cascade mux select and enable controls are also clocked; the Vivado 2025.1 UNISIM models explicitly sample CASDOMUX and CASOREGIMUX controls at the corresponding clock edge. Cascade data/error inputs remain conservative bypass dependencies. Unknown controls remain conservative. The implementation was checked against the installed 2025.1 RAM32M, RAM32M16, RAM64M8, SRL16E, SRLC32E, RAMB18E2, RAMB36E2, and DSP48E1/E2 simulation models. [AMD RAMB36E2 port reference](https://docs.amd.com/r/2020.2-English/ug974-vivado-ultrascale-libraries/RAMB36E2).

A DSP48E1/E2 is a whole-cell sequential barrier only when PREG is explicitly one and every connected output belongs to its registered P/status/cascade-output group. Connected ACOUT/BCOUT, a missing PREG attribute, and unknown DSP types retain conservative combinational behavior. [AMD DSP48E2 register description](https://docs.amd.com/api/khub/documents/pTysoma4TYgNH95BrY1Sbw/content), [AMD DSP48E1 attribute reference](https://docs.amd.com/r/2023.2-English/ug953-vivado-7series-libraries/DSP48E1).

This distinction mattered on the actual OpenTitan input: an unchanged k4 assignment initially had 2,293 cyclic nodes in the coarse dependency model. Separating LUTRAM/SRL writes reduced that to 2,029; retaining only connected LUTRAM read ports and separating ordinary BRAM inputs reduced it to 1,178; identifying clocked E2 cascade controls removed the remaining false SCC. The final audit covered 435,483 timing nodes, including 817 synthetic capture endpoints, with zero cyclic or blocked nodes. These are improvements to model fidelity on the same mapping, not partition-quality improvements or a proof that all hardware timing arcs are exact. Counterexample tests preserve real asynchronous address-feedback cycles, cascade-data feedthrough, and dynamic SRL address transit.

### Native packing feedback and balance

TritonPart's timing-aware objective and MFSPart's resource/topology constraints still require a truthful weight model. A logical LUT primitive count is exact as a census but does not predict how Vivado combines LUT functions into physical CLB LUTs. On SHA4, logically balanced mappings exhibited much larger native packed-area imbalance. An exploratory nonnegative six-feature LUT1..LUT6 fit reduced same-design leave-one-partition-out mean absolute error to 1.55%, but its coefficients included LUT6 above two and LUT3/LUT4 near 0.12. Those values reflect correlated structural mixtures and omitted packing interactions, not literal physical costs. Good held-out prediction on existing partitions does not certify weights after the optimizer changes those mixtures. [TritonPart](https://vlsicad.ucsd.edu/Publications/Conferences/401/c401.pdf), [MFSPart](https://zhiyaoxie.com/files/TCAD26_MFSPart.pdf).

The optional calibrated primitive and frontier weight files are therefore estimates with checked source identity; frontier feedback additionally binds every vertex's exact hierarchy path. The original logical resource guard remains independent and its relaxation is explicit. Mt-KaHyPar v1.6.2 supplies scalar balance or per-FPGA scalar capacities; the refiner preserves the same configured limits, using floor((1+epsilon)*ceil(total/k)) for homogeneous integer bounds. Native per-partition Vivado utilization is the final authority for actual CLB limits and achieved physical balance. A native cap pass, a met balance target, complete path analysis, and cut/topology guards are separate outcomes. None establishes final placement, routing, transport overhead, or achievable DUT frequency.

## Implemented sampled native timing guidance

`TimingPathModel` now reads source-bound, ordered Vivado setup paths in addition to the all-modeled-path structural audit. The TSV binds the input EDIF SHA256 and preserves each path's source slack s, positive requirement R, launch clock, capture clock, and ordered cells. An ordinary unmapped cell rejects the sample. Physical macro children resolve to their longest matching logical frontier ancestor; consecutive repeated owners compress, while nonconsecutive reentries remain. Explicit top-level I/O markers have no assigned FPGA home and add no invented boundary crossing. Missing or nonpositive requirements do not acquire an invented clock period: those rows are counted as unscored.

For a scored path p, source criticality is q(p) = max(0, 1 − s(p)/R(p))². The fixed source normalization Q is the largest q over the exported sample, or one when all source criticalities are zero. This preserves relative importance while making integer edge weights useful even when the unchanged source clock is loose. For example, the verified SHA sample uses a 100 ns source requirement and has maximum q approximately 0.001865; rounding 8q directly would give every native edge unit weight. Normalizing by Q makes the most critical sampled owner-pair eligible for a ninefold weight at timing weight eight. This is relative criticality, not a conversion to nanoseconds or a claim that the source design runs at the platform's eventual DUT frequency.

Initial Mt-KaHyPar v1.6.2 input uses hMETIS weighted-edge/weighted-vertex format 11. Each hyperedge receives 1 + round(W × max(q(p)/Q)) over sampled consecutive driver/sink owner pairs associated with that net. A cell-only sample cannot distinguish parallel nets between the same owners; all matching pairs are therefore weighted conservatively. Unmatched edges keep unit weight. These optimization weights do not change the separately reported unweighted transfer counts, resource census, or hard capacity checks. [Pinned v1.6.2 file formats](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kahypar/mt-kahypar/v1.6.2/mt-kahypar/io/docs/FileFormats.md).

The refiner separately evaluates each sampled path in the current assignment. Let c be its logical crossing count, H its declared minimum-link hop sum and r its logical block reentries. The default sampled cost is (q/Q) × [H + max(0,H−1)² + r]; without declared adjacency the legacy severity remains c. Local moves recompute costs on incident sampled paths. Retained passes apply the selected quality guard separately from resource, occupancy, fixed-assignment and cut/topology checks. Without an explicit crossing-delay model, ranked mode compares the all-modeled-path maximum H first, then maximum logical crossings, sampled timing cost, endpoint excess and transit. A primary H/C improvement may permit greater sampled timing cost under that declared order; componentwise mode forbids it. Source criticality alone cannot establish transport-delay dominance, and the exported sample can omit a worse register-free path. Clock names and requirements remain attached to each path; they are not replaced with a common fabricated period.

No inter-FPGA delay is assumed by default. If the user explicitly supplies a per-logical-crossing extra delay δ, a sampled path's modeled slack becomes s′ = s − δc and its criticality is recomputed from s′ after every proposed move. This continues to multiply logical c, not H. Eligible candidates then prefer higher modeled worst sampled slack, followed by lower sampled timing cost, then H, C, endpoint excess and transit. Ranked retained-pass acceptance follows that exact order: an improvement in modeled worst slack may permit a secondary timing-cost or structural regression. Componentwise mode requires both timing metrics and each structural measure to remain nonregressing. This catches an exported path that was initially noncritical but becomes critical under the explicit crossing model. The reported value is labeled modeled slack and is not emitted as an achieved routed frequency.

An actual SHA seed comparison motivated this conditional priority before the H extension. One source-only timing winner had 1,176 transfers, maximum logical crossings four, and normalized sampled cost 74.44; another had 1,076 transfers, maximum logical crossings three, and sampled cost 124.40. Both were inside the allowed cut budget. The source clock was still 100 ns, with roughly 95.7 ns slack and no supplied transport delay. Selecting four global crossings solely from that sampled cost would contradict the user's preference against severe unregistered transit. That version chose the three-crossing candidate, using the actual STA sample to guide equal-maximum alternatives and local moves. Current declared-topology ranking places H first, then logical crossings before sampled cost; this historical comparison makes no retrospective H claim. Explicit transport-delay mode can make the sampled modeled timing comparison primary, with its assumptions reported. Regression fixtures preserve the mode distinction and complete-J4 equivalence.

This is a concrete timing-guided step inspired by TritonPart, with a narrower guarantee: there is no complete STA graph propagation, no extraction of partitioned interconnect delays, and no new discovery of paths absent from the exported sample. TritonPart propagates cut-induced timing changes through a complete timing graph; our current method reevaluates only the supplied native paths. The exact structural DAG audit still covers every modeled primitive path independently of timing sampling. A complete structural audit does not imply complete STA coverage. [TritonPart timing model and refinement](https://vlsicad.ucsd.edu/Publications/Conferences/401/c401.pdf), [OpenROAD partition timing options](https://openroad.readthedocs.io/en/latest/main/src/par/README.html).

The fresh Vivado 2025.1 SHA export was verified end to end with 1,000 finite constrained setup paths under the original platform clock, no skipped rows in that sample, and unknown design-wide unconstrained-path count. Its source worst slack was 95.681 ns. The installed tool's supported `get_pins -of_objects $timing_path` query supplied ordered data pins without the clock tree; an independent native `report_timing` check reproduced the first path's 38 pins and 20 cells in order. The installed 2025.1 timing-path object does not expose a `TIMING_POINTS` property, so the exporter does not rely on it. Loading the verified TSV against an existing k4 SHA assignment scored all 1,000 rows, resolved 1,941 directed owner pairs, and left modeled worst slack absent when no δ was supplied. These checks establish input/order/model integration, not a measured partitioned timing improvement.

## Refinement sequence and meaningful experiments

1. Build a hierarchy frontier small enough that every indivisible vertex fits at least one FPGA and enough real vertices exist for requested occupancy. Adaptive splitting is preferable to unconditional full flattening.
2. Run pinned v1.6.2 with multiple seeds and at least connectivity and topology-aware objective variants where available. Record executable identity/hash, graph hash, constraints, preset, seed, threads, and runtime.
3. Repair resource or occupancy violations before optimization. A greedy move should strictly reduce a defined infeasibility measure, not merely reduce cut cost. Full destinations may require swaps or smaller frontier vertices; failure to repair is not proof the design is infeasible.
4. Refine boundary vertices and small disjoint cones using net-local cost updates plus bounded directed witness costs. Restrict candidate moves to feasible destinations; use pairs/swaps to cross tight-balance local minima. Recompute witnesses between passes because a previously unimportant path can become worst.
5. Perform the full DAG audit after a proposed pass. Roll back candidates violating feasibility, the transfer budget, or explicitly promised path-quality bounds. Report the native result, repaired baseline, and final result separately.
6. Compare 4/16/24/28 partitions with complete, mesh/ring, and asymmetric-link topologies. Include a held-out design family. An improvement on a replicated SHA datapath alone does not establish performance on OpenTitan control logic or NVDLA.

The first evaluation should contain counterexamples: A→B→C with no reentry, A→B→A, an actual sequential capture between crossings, fanout shared within one FPGA, reconvergent paths, an opaque primitive, a combinational SCC with acyclic descendants, a zero-resource virtual-only FPGA, fixed assignments, and asymmetric resource bottlenecks. Exhaustive path enumeration on tiny DAGs independently checks the linear-time audit. Large-netlist metrics establish scalability and measurable tradeoffs; they do not establish achieved frequency without the downstream implementation flow that is outside this task.
